# Segment and cohort analysis

Generated by `python scripts/segment_analysis.py` from the queries in `sql/`, run with DuckDB directly on `WA_Fn-UseC_-Telco-Customer-Churn.csv` (all 7,043 customers, not only the test split). Churn rate intervals are 95% Wilson score intervals.

**Every finding below is correlational, not causal.** The dataset is a single observational snapshot with no intervention, so a segment with high churn shows where to look, not what would change the outcome.

## Findings

1. Churn is concentrated early in the customer lifetime. Customers in their first 6 months churn at 52.9% (95% CI 50.4% to 55.5%), against 9.5% (95% CI 8.4% to 10.8%) for customers past 48 months (overall rate 26.5%). This is correlational, not causal.
2. Month-to-month customers paying by electronic check are the largest high-risk segment: 1,850 customers (26.3% of the base) churn at 53.7% (95% CI 51.5% to 56.0%) and account for 53.2% of all churners. This is correlational, not causal.
3. Fiber optic customers without online security and without tech support churn at 55.0% (95% CI 52.7% to 57.3%), while DSL customers with both churn at 6.3% (95% CI 4.8% to 8.4%). Customers who choose add-on services may differ in other ways, so this does not show that selling the add-ons would reduce churn. This is correlational, not causal.
4. Churn rises with monthly charges but is not strictly monotonic. The lowest charge band (average 20.68 per month) churns at 9.2% (95% CI 7.8% to 10.8%), band 4 (average 86.00) is highest at 36.1% (95% CI 33.6% to 38.6%), and the top band (average 103.16) is 32.8% (95% CI 30.4% to 35.3%). This is correlational, not causal.
5. Month-to-month contracts hold 86.9% of the monthly revenue lost to churn (120,847.10 of 139,130.85 per month), so retention spend aimed at this group covers most of the revenue at risk. This is correlational, not causal.
